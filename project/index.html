What Is Disc Golf?

Disc golf is a sport where players throw flying discs from a tee area toward a metal basket, trying to complete each hole in the fewest throws possible. This site introduces the basics of the game, the types of discs players use, and some of the top players in the sport today.

Where to Find a Place to Play

One reason disc golf is so easy to try is that many courses are located in public parks, and a lot of them are free to play. Compared to many other sports, you do not need expensive equipment, reserved court time, or a large group to get started. With just a disc and a nearby course, it is easy for beginners to play a round on their own schedule. Check UDisc for courses near you.

Basic Rules

  • Each hole begins with a throw from the tee area.
  • Players throw toward the basket until the disc lands in the target.
  • Every throw counts as one stroke.
  • The player farthest from the basket throws next.
  • The goal is to finish each hole in the fewest throws possible.
  • The player with the lowest total score at the end of the round wins.
  • Players usually continue from the spot where their previous throw landed.
  • Most courses have 9 or 18 holes that are played in order.

    Disc Types Preview

    Putters

    Putters are designed for short, controlled throws and are most often used when aiming for the basket. They usually have a slower speed and a straighter flight, which helps players focus on accuracy.

    Midranges

    Midranges are useful for throws that need more distance than a putter but still need good control. They are a common choice for approach shots and for players who want a reliable, balanced flight.

    Drivers

    Drivers are made for longer throws and are typically used off the tee or on open fairways. They can travel farther than other discs, but they usually require more speed and control to throw well.

    Why Try Disc Golf?

    Disc golf is a great sport to try because it is affordable, easy to start, and enjoyable for many different skill levels. A beginner can get started with just one or two discs, and many courses are free to play. It is also a good way to spend time outside, get exercise, and enjoy friendly competition whether you are playing alone or with friends. You can also check Disc Golf Scene to find local clubs that share free leagues, events, and tournaments to play in or spectate.
